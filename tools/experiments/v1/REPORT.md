# EMBERBench V1 evaluation pack: report

**PUBLIC DEVELOPMENT EVALUATION — NOT AN OFFICIAL HIDDEN-SUITE SCORE**

This report shows how EMBERBench compares decision-making methods with the official EMBER Score (`emberbench.scoring`, scoring version 1.0). Every method is scored against one frozen environment pack of the **public** V1 suite, with one episode per **unique environment world**. The scoring procedure is official; the suite is public and known, so these numbers are development results. They are not hidden-suite scores, not leaderboard-grade generalization evidence and they can be overfit.

Generated by `tools/experiments/v1/run_public_benchmark.py` from authenticated artifacts only.

## 10. Limitations

- **Public worlds can be overfit.** The suite and the candidate seeds are public, and the pack agents were designed while looking at them. Nothing here predicts hidden-suite scores.
- **Only one scenario has stochastic fire** (`v1-pub-10-route-invalidation`). The other nine scenarios are one world each, whatever the seed.
- **This is not the hidden 10-family suite.** The hidden V1 design (docs/evaluation-suite-design.md) draws about 16 scenarios per family from a generated pool; the public suite is a fixed development suite.
- **Public-family coverage is shallow:** one scenario per primary family, so a family score is one scenario score, and family intervals cannot be estimated.
- **Score precision here is not the precision of the final 240-world hidden design.** The intervals reflect only the fire-world variation of one stochastic scenario; scenario-sampling variance is not represented at all. The hidden design expects about +-4 points overall (docs/evaluation-suite-design.md, section 11).
- Congestion diagnostics are shown separately and are not score components.
- The privileged references ignore other people (M*) or relax capacity to intervals (M_cap), so a gap to them is not all recoverable by any policy.
- The forecast of the pack agents uses only the public rules in the observation. For deterministic fire it is exact, so on the nine deterministic scenarios the future fire is predictable to every fair method.
- Wall time, tokens and cost are not part of any pinned artifact (they are not reproducible); see `tools/experiments/v1/README.md` for the measured runtime.
- The agents are in-process Python code. The fairness guarantee is the interface contract (docs/evaluation.md, section 4), not a sandbox.
